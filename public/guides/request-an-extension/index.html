Need more time? Ask before the deadline.

A specific plan is more useful than “I’ll get to it.” Tell the officer what is scheduled, what is holding up the work and the date you can realistically finish.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Contact the officer listed on your notice before the compliance date. Ask who has authority to extend it, propose a specific completion date, attach supporting proof and request a written response. Approval is not automatic.

Start with the assigned officer

Use the officer’s direct contact information on the notice. Have the case number and property address ready. If you cannot reach the officer, contact the issuing department and ask who is covering the case. A cleanup company can supply a work estimate and schedule; it cannot grant an extension.

Tell the office whether this is an initial violation deadline, a deadline in a board or Magistrate order, a court response date or a government-abatement notice. The person who can change one date may not have authority to change another.

If an order has already been entered, ask whether an extension requires a motion, application or another hearing. Do not assume a phone conversation changes a written order.

Send a short, documented plan

  • The case number, address and your relationship to the property.
  • The specific date you are requesting, not an open-ended delay.
  • A contractor estimate or confirmed work date, if available.
  • Dated photos of work already completed and remaining conditions.
  • A factual explanation of the obstacle: access, documented illness, estate authority, equipment availability or unsafe ground conditions.
  • A plan to remove debris and prevent the same condition returning.

Keep personal documentation relevant and limited. Ask the agency how to submit sensitive information before sending medical or financial records. A promise from a contractor is not proof that the government has accepted a new deadline.

A plain-English extension request

Subject: Extension request — Case [number], [property address]

I am responsible for the property at [address]. The notice lists a compliance deadline of [date]. I am arranging [specific cleanup work] and request an extension through [new date] because [brief factual reason].

[Contractor/work crew] is scheduled for [date]. I have attached [estimate, scheduling confirmation or progress photos]. The remaining work is [list].

Please let me know whether you can approve this request or whether a formal request to the board, Magistrate or court is required. Please confirm the applicable deadline in writing and tell me whether the scheduled hearing or any fines will be affected.

If you do not get an answer

Follow up before the original deadline and document the date and method of each contact. Continue the work you can safely and lawfully complete. The existing deadline remains your working deadline until an authorized decision changes it.

If your request is denied, ask what can still be corrected on time and whether staged completion is acceptable. If a hearing is scheduled, prepare to explain the work done, the reason for delay and your remaining plan. Bring the correspondence; do not skip the hearing because you asked for more time.

If a court response or appeal deadline is involved, contact a Florida lawyer or the relevant court promptly. This cleanup guide does not calculate or extend legal filing deadlines.

Coordinate the cleanup with the revised deadline

Once a work date is confirmed, share it with the officer and ask how to request reinspection. Arrange access, identify disposal needs and photograph the initial condition. After completion, send dated photos and the invoice. Ask for the recorded compliance date and case status.

If the deadline is within seven days or has passed, call 813-671-2757 to discuss cleanup availability. Availability and the required scope must be confirmed before a job is scheduled.
