The yard is clear. What about the lien?

Cleanup, a zero balance and a released lien are three different milestones. Keep going until the government confirms what remains and the public record reflects any required release.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Compliance does not automatically erase an existing fine or recorded lien. Request the current balance, identify the local reduction procedure, submit supporting records and obtain written approval. Confirm payment and a recorded release where required.

How a code enforcement lien can attach

Under § 162.09(3), recording a certified fine order can create a lien against the violation property and other real or personal property owned by the violator. The effect on a particular owner or property can involve legal exemptions and title questions.

Government-abatement charges may instead arise under a local mowing or nuisance-assessment procedure. Ask whether your balance is an administrative fine lien, an abatement assessment, a court judgment or more than one obligation. The same reduction rules do not necessarily apply to each.

Get the case number, recording information and an itemized payoff. A database entry marked “complied” does not necessarily establish that the debt is paid or the lien released.

Request a reduction or mitigation review

Section 162.09(2)(c) allows a board to reduce a fine. Local rules determine how to apply, who decides and what documentation is required. A cleanup company can document its work but cannot promise a settlement or act as your lawyer.

  1. Complete all cited corrective work and get the compliance determination.
  2. Request the reduction application and an itemized balance.
  3. Collect before/after photos, invoices, dates and relevant communications.
  4. Explain the circumstances concisely and document corrective effort.
  5. Submit the required signed or notarized materials through the official channel.
  6. Get the decision, payment conditions and release instructions in writing.

Do not assume a requested percentage will be approved. Enforcement costs, abatement bills and interest may receive different treatment from the fine.

Two local processes to know

Hillsborough County

The county’s settlement instructions require compliance before settlement or release. The property owner or authorized representative submits a complete, signed application with notarized signatures through the official submission channel. Qualifying homesteaded hardship situations may receive consideration with supporting documents. Read the county’s current instructions for where and how to submit.

Pinellas County

The county’s reduction page says a compliant property owner may be eligible and also provides a route for a prospective new owner of a noncompliant property. That does not guarantee a reduction or mean a buyer can ignore outstanding work. Submit the requested facts and wait for the county’s determination.

For a city case, use that city’s procedure. A county’s reduction form does not settle a Tampa, Clearwater, St. Petersburg or Bradenton lien merely because the property lies within the county.

Sales, refinancing and inherited property

Tell your closing agent or title professional about every known notice, order and lien early. Obtain a current payoff and ask whether additional cases, recorded orders or assessments exist. A search for one case number is not a complete title examination.

If ownership is being transferred while an enforcement proceeding is pending, § 162.06(5) imposes specific disclosure and notification duties in the situation it covers. Get transaction-specific advice; a sale does not automatically dismiss the proceeding.

Do not promise a closing date based solely on the cleanup date. Reinspection, reduction review, payment processing and recording a release can each take additional time.

Foreclosure risk—and the homestead distinction

Section 162.09(3) allows the board, after an unpaid lien has been recorded for three months, to authorize the local government’s attorney to pursue foreclosure or a money judgment. It is not automatic foreclosure on day 91.

The same statute bars foreclosure of a Part I lien on constitutionally protected homestead property and limits the money-judgment provision for constitutionally protected property. Do not assume that protection cancels the debt, releases the lien or necessarily governs a different type of local assessment.

Section 162.10 generally limits a Part I lien to 20 years from recording, with a litigation exception. Do not calculate your rights from that headline alone. Get a Florida lawyer’s advice if you receive a foreclosure complaint, collection lawsuit or disputed title report.

Verify the finish line

After an approved settlement or full payment, keep the payment receipt and obtain the satisfaction or release. Verify the recording information with the issuing office or your title professional. Ask separately whether the enforcement case is closed and whether recurring inspection or maintenance obligations remain.

Arrange ongoing mowing so new growth does not create another case while a settlement is being processed.
