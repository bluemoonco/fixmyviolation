What happens at a code enforcement hearing?

A hearing is your opportunity to respond to the alleged violation and put your evidence in the record. Prepare even if the cleanup has already been completed.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Read the notice to identify the hearing body, date, location and submission rules. Bring the notice, a short chronology, dated photos, invoices and correspondence. Do not assume a cleanup or extension request cancels your appearance.

Board, Magistrate or court?

A Code Enforcement Board is a panel. A Special Magistrate is an individual appointed to hear administrative code cases; § 162.03(2) allows an alternative system with the board’s status and powers. Neither should be confused with the judge hearing a county-court citation.

Build a small, organized evidence packet

  1. Notice and case details: all pages, attachments, the address and each cited section.
  2. One-page chronology: when you learned of the notice, contacted the officer, arranged work and completed it.
  3. Before/after photographs: wide views and close views of every cited condition, with reliable dates and matching angles.
  4. Invoice and work description: the property address, work performed and completion date.
  5. Correspondence: extension decisions, access issues, progress reports and any reinspection findings.

Ask how and when to submit digital files, whether printed copies are required, and whether remote attendance or an authorized representative is allowed. Do not assume you can simply display photos from your phone. An entity or estate may have representation issues that warrant legal advice.

During and after the hearing

Under § 162.07, testimony is under oath and recorded. The officer and alleged violator can present testimony. Formal evidence rules do not apply, but fundamental due process must be observed. The decision results in findings and an order.

Address each cited condition calmly. Explain what was completed, identify the evidence and make a specific request if more time is needed. If you disagree with the allegation, identify the factual or code issue instead of assuming recent mowing resolves every dispute.

Get the written order. Confirm any new compliance date, daily fine, cost assessment and reinspection requirement. A late correction can still leave the case eligible for a hearing under § 162.06(2); ask the office whether attendance is still required.

Appeal rights have a short clock

Part I administrative appeal: § 162.11 provides an appeal to circuit court within 30 days of execution of the order. Review is based on the administrative record; it is not a new trial.

Do not measure this deadline from the day you happen to read the order without checking the applicable law. A fine-reduction request or a call to the officer is not a substitute for filing an appeal. Do not assume enforcement is stayed merely because you are considering or filing one.

Contact a Florida lawyer promptly if you wish to challenge an order, dispute service, preserve an appellate issue or seek a stay. A county-court judgment follows its own appeal procedures; do not apply the administrative appeal rule indiscriminately.
