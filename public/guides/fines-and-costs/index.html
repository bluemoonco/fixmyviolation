How code enforcement fines add up.

A daily fine, a civil citation and a bill for government mowing are different things. Start by identifying the charge and the order that authorizes it.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Under Florida’s standard Part I limits, fines can be up to $250 per day for a first violation and $500 per day for a repeat violation. Qualifying governments can adopt higher limits. These are legal ceilings, not an automatic price for an overgrown yard.

The state framework is not a local price list

Florida Statutes § 162.09(2): administrative fine limits
AuthorityFirst violationRepeat violationIrreparable or irreversible
Standard limits, § 162.09(2)(a)Up to $250/dayUp to $500/dayUp to $5,000/violation
Locally adopted higher authority, § 162.09(2)(d)Up to $1,000/day/violationUp to $5,000/day/violationUp to $15,000/violation

The second row is available only where a government with at least 50,000 residents has adopted the required ordinance by the specified vote. Population alone does not establish a local fine schedule. Read § 162.09(2) for the conditions.

The amount actually imposed depends on the local authority and the order in your case. Ordinary overgrowth that can be corrected should not simply be assumed to be an irreversible violation. A special historic-demolition provision in § 162.09(2)(e) is outside this mowing guide.

When the daily clock starts and stops

For an ordinary first violation under Part I, § 162.09(1) ties daily fines to continued noncompliance after the board’s ordered date. For a repeat violation, fines may run from the date the inspector finds that it occurred. A missed courtesy deadline is not, by itself, proof that a particular daily fine has already been imposed.

Under § 162.09(3), accrual continues until compliance or judgment in the specified collection suit, whichever comes first. Promptly document the cleanup and obtain the government’s written compliance determination. Ask which date it recognizes; do not rely on a contractor’s completion date alone to settle a disputed balance.

Example only: If an order sets $100 per day and the counted noncompliance period is 12 days, the daily-fine component is $1,200. This is arithmetic, not a quoted local fine. Costs and other charges may be additional.

A repeat case is not just a second warning

Section 162.04(5) defines a Part I repeat violation by reference to a previous finding of a violation of the same code provision within five years. It can involve a different location. Simply receiving two complaint letters is not the complete legal test.

The repeat-case procedures can dispense with another reasonable correction period. A civil-citation program can also have its own local repeat definition, so check the enforcement track rather than importing the Part I definition into every ticket.

The balance can include more than a fine

  • Enforcement costs: Part I provides for recovery of prosecution costs when the government prevails, and applicable local provisions may authorize additional costs.
  • Abatement costs: Contractor mowing, debris removal and authorized administrative charges can create a separate bill.
  • Recording, release and collection costs: Lien-related expenses may apply under the relevant authority.
  • Interest: Confirm whether it applies to your particular lien or assessment and how a payoff is calculated.

Request an itemized balance that separates daily fines, costs, interest and abatement charges. A payment for one case may not satisfy another case at the same address.

Cleanup and fine reduction are separate steps

Correcting the violation helps stop the problem from getting more expensive, but it does not automatically wipe out the existing balance. Section 162.09(2)(c) permits fine reduction; it does not promise one. Follow the local application and approval process.

Keep photos, invoices, scheduling records and your communications with the officer. Those records help establish what was corrected and when. See liens and fine reductions for preparing a reduction request.
