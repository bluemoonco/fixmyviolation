What counts as an overgrown-property violation?

A yard can violate local rules because of its height, its condition, or what it blocks. Start with the ordinance on your notice—not a height limit from a neighboring city.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

There is no single Tampa Bay grass-height rule. The issuing city or county sets the standard. A cleanup may need to address grass, underbrush, debris and an adjoining right-of-way, not just the front lawn.

Height is only part of the rule

Some local rules use a measurable height; others also address untended vegetation, vermin, fire hazards or obstructed access. A maintained landscape bed is not necessarily treated like an abandoned patch of weeds. The plant type, the portion of the lot affected and any applicable exception matter.

For example, Pinellas County’s official summary describes grass and weeds reaching 12 inches over the majority of the property. Clearwater cites § 3-1503.B.7 for growth over 12 inches. Manatee County’s guidance says to keep grass below 16 inches. These are different local rules, not interchangeable mowing targets.

Read the exact section, definitions and exceptions that apply to your parcel. Our jurisdiction guides identify the official code and enforcement office. Where we cannot confirm a current numeric limit, we do not publish a guessed one.

Weeds, brush and debris can be separate violations

Vines on fencing, dense underbrush, fallen limbs and accumulated yard waste may require more than a standard lawn cut. A notice can list both an overgrowth section and a trash or debris section. Finishing one item does not necessarily resolve the other.

  • Read every cited item and inspect the back and side yards as well as the street frontage.
  • Identify debris hidden in the growth before equipment enters the property.
  • Ask what must be removed, what may remain as mulch and how waste must be placed for authorized collection.
  • Do not move the problem to the street, a drainage ditch or an adjacent lot.

Tell the cleanup crew about wire, concrete, dumped trash, stumps, soft ground and other obstacles. A bush hog cuts vegetation; it is not a substitute for collecting hidden debris.

Vacant does not mean exempt

A vacant house still has a yard to maintain. An undeveloped lot may be subject to different rules from a developed residential parcel, and large, agricultural, conservation or environmentally sensitive parcels may require a closer look at scope and exceptions.

Before ordering an entire wooded lot cleared, ask the officer which area and which vegetation must be addressed. An overgrowth notice is not blanket permission to remove protected trees, disturb wetlands, alter drainage or clear a conservation area. Keep those questions separate from ordinary mowing.

Check the sidewalk, swale and right-of-way

The required maintenance area may extend beyond a fence or surveyed property line. Some jurisdictions place maintenance duties on the abutting owner or occupant. Other areas belong to an agency or are subject to easements. Ask the officer to identify the exact strip on a photo or parcel map.

Keep sidewalks passable and ask about sight-line issues at corners. Do not fill or regrade a swale to make mowing easier. If access is unsafe or boundaries are uncertain, document the condition and request clarification before work.

Turn the notice into a cleanup scope

  1. Record the government’s name, case number, cited sections and deadline.
  2. Photograph each affected area, including side yards and any cited frontage.
  3. Confirm the work area, disposal requirements and any protected features.
  4. Arrange mowing, brush clearing and debris removal as needed.
  5. Send completion evidence and request reinspection and written closure.

We can quote the cleanup and provide before/after documentation. The enforcing government decides whether the cited conditions comply.
