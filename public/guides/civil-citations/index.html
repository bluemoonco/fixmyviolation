A citation is different from a cleanup notice.

If your paperwork tells you to pay a civil penalty, contest a citation or appear in court, deal with those instructions separately from getting the yard cut.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

A code violation does not automatically become a court case after a set number of days. Local government may use a civil-citation process under Chapter 162 Part II. Your citation identifies how to pay or contest it and the applicable deadline.

Identify which document you actually have

DocumentWhat it usually asks you to do
Notice of violationCorrect stated conditions by a specified date.
Administrative hearing noticeAppear before a board or Special Magistrate.
Civil citationFollow the stated payment or contest procedure for an alleged civil infraction.
Notice to appear, summons or court noticeFollow the specified court appearance or response instructions.

Read the document’s heading, issuing agency, ordinance, case number and forum. A notice can mention several consequences, so if the track is unclear, call the office identified on it and the court clerk when a court case number is listed.

What Florida’s civil-citation statute says

Section 162.21(3) permits an authorized officer to issue a citation, based on personal investigation and reasonable cause, for a civil infraction that county court will hear. Before an ordinary citation, the officer gives notice and a reasonable correction period of no more than 30 days. Repeat, serious-threat and irreversible conditions have exceptions.

“No more than 30 days” is a ceiling in that procedure—not a promise that every property owner gets 30 days. Follow the actual deadline. Local government must adopt the applicable implementation and penalty provisions.

Under § 162.21(5), this civil-infraction procedure has a maximum civil penalty of $500 and a lesser penalty for not contesting. Check the local schedule and citation for the amount and applicable costs; this is not the same as the daily administrative fine limits in § 162.09.

Local examples of different enforcement tracks

Tampa Code Chapter 23.5 expressly implements Part II and places violations governed by that chapter in county court. It sets local citation procedures and classified penalties. Use the exact provision on the ticket; do not assume one citywide fine applies to every vegetation issue.

Bradenton’s Code Enforcement Division says it uses both a Special Magistrate and the citation process. Manatee County’s case-process page describes citations, notices to appear and referral to a Special Magistrate as possible routes. These are alternatives or related steps, not proof that every case follows one fixed ladder.

Respond even if you have already mowed

Cutting the grass does not automatically withdraw a citation already issued. Payment, a contest request, a dismissal and administrative compliance are separate actions. Ask for written confirmation of any withdrawal and check court status when a case has been filed.

Under § 162.21, failing to pay or appear as required can waive the right to contest and allow judgment up to the maximum civil penalty. Read the instructions immediately. If you plan to contest liability, seek legal advice before choosing a payment option with consequences you do not understand.

Keep the citation, photographs, invoice and all communications. Do not send a contractor to a hearing assuming that they can represent you.

Take a notice to appear seriously

Section 162.23 addresses a distinct notice-to-appear procedure. It generally calls for a written correction notice with a period of at least 5 and no more than 30 days before a notice to appear, subject to its stated exceptions. That rule should not be confused with § 162.21’s civil-citation provisions.

Part II also includes municipal-ordinance enforcement provisions beyond civil infractions. A court document should not be dismissed as “just a mowing ticket.” Confirm the nature of the proceeding and required response with the court and, where appropriate, a Florida lawyer.
