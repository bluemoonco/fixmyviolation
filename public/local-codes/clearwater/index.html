Clearwater overgrowth and nuisance notices.

Clearwater’s Community Development Code addresses overgrowth and unmaintained rights-of-way as distinct nuisance conditions. Check whether your notice includes both.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Clearwater § 3-1503.B.7 addresses excessive growth over 12 inches and debris accumulation. Section 3-1503.B.8 separately addresses unmaintained abutting rights-of-way. Follow the city’s notice for the exact areas and deadline.

The city’s nuisance sections

In the Clearwater Community Development Code, § 3-1503.B.7 covers excessive weeds, grass, undergrowth or similar plant material reaching more than 12 inches, as well as accumulation of debris. The city’s 2026 enforcement affidavit quotes that provision and § 3-1503.B.8 for unmaintained right-of-way conditions.

This is a city standard, not the Pinellas County standard for unincorporated land. Confirm the issuing government even when the mailing address is Clearwater.

Ask which specific conditions remain if the yard has already been partly mowed. Debris, an unmaintained strip or another cited nuisance can keep the case open after most of the grass is cut.

Include the abutting maintenance area

Review the sidewalk, curbside strip and other frontage mentioned on the notice. Ask the inspector where the required area starts and ends, especially if the parcel adjoins multiple streets or the boundary is unclear.

Do not assume mowing inside the fence is enough. At the same time, do not cut a neighbor’s property or alter a drainage area without confirming authority and scope. Photograph any access, utility or drainage obstacle and raise it before the deadline.

Municipal Code Enforcement Board and other routes

Clearwater publishes a Municipal Code Enforcement Board process. Its citizen handbook also identifies Special Magistrate and court enforcement options. Read the actual hearing or citation notice to identify your forum and response requirements.

The city’s enforcement guidance explains that case timing varies with the owner’s response and hearing schedules. There is no single guaranteed resolution time. Ask the assigned inspector about an extension before the correction date if you cannot complete the required scope.

Use the current meeting notice for the location and time. Bring completion photographs and the invoice, and verify whether the hearing is still required after the cleanup.

A practical Clearwater response

  1. Match the address and case number to the city notice.
  2. Mark each item under § 3-1503, including any right-of-way item.
  3. Confirm the mowing, brush and debris-removal scope.
  4. Arrange access and a work date; contact the inspector if timing is a problem.
  5. Send dated before/after photos and request written reinspection findings.

If the city has already assessed fines or recorded a lien, ask for a current balance and the applicable reduction or release process. A clean property is evidence of correction, not automatic proof of a zero balance.
