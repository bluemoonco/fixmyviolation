Manatee County overgrown-yard violations.

For Parrish, Ellenton and other county locations, check the parcel and the notice. Bradenton and Palmetto are separate municipalities; a postal address alone does not decide which rules apply.

By Fix My ViolationLast reviewed: October 7, 2026

The short answer

Manatee County’s official guidance says grass must be kept under 16 inches and links to § 2-9-103 in its Property Maintenance and Structural Standards article. Use the exact cited provision and notice to confirm the affected area, exceptions and correction deadline.

The county’s height guidance and code definitions

Manatee County’s Code Enforcement Do’s and Don’ts tells owners to keep grass under 16 inches. Its overgrown-yard link points to § 2-9-103, definitions in Chapter 2-9, Article VI. Read that article together with the substantive section cited on your notice.

The same official guidance treats trash, debris and outdoor storage as separate concerns. An overgrown parcel may have more than one item to correct. Do not assume a mowing pass resolves a storage or debris violation.

For a large or partly natural lot, ask the officer about the area to be maintained and applicable exceptions. An overgrowth case is not blanket authorization to disturb wetlands, conservation areas or protected trees.

County enforcement serves unincorporated areas

The county’s Code Enforcement 101 page describes its unincorporated service area and distinguishes code enforcement from HOA enforcement. Verify the issuing government before choosing an office or code standard.

Use the assigned officer’s number on the notice for case questions. Give the parcel number if the location is a vacant lot, has no clear street number or is close to a municipal boundary. For cleanup service, our number is 813-671-2757.

The county explains several possible enforcement paths

Manatee’s published case-process guidance describes an initial courtesy notice, often a door hanger, followed by a formal notice if the condition is not resolved. It then identifies a citation, a notice to appear in court or a referral to a Special Magistrate as possible next steps.

That local description does not guarantee a courtesy stage in every circumstance. Repeat violations and serious conditions can be handled differently under state law. Follow the date printed on the actual document rather than assuming a standard grace period.

The county’s process page gives examples of daily fine amounts after Magistrate proceedings. Those examples are not a substitute for the rate in your own order or the applicable legal limits. Request the order and an itemized balance if fines are already running.

Prepare for the Special Magistrate

Manatee County’s Special Magistrate holds hearings and can impose fines under the county’s enforcement system. If a hearing is scheduled, prepare the notice, a dated work chronology, before/after photos and invoices.

Ask about extensions before the compliance date. If a Magistrate order already sets the date, find out whether a formal request must be heard. Keep attending to the original deadline unless an authorized decision changes it.

Get a written compliance determination

Send the officer the completed-work photos and invoice with the address and case number. Request reinspection, written compliance status and the effective compliance date. Confirm whether any court appearance or administrative hearing remains.

For an outstanding fine or lien, request the current county instructions for reduction, payment and release. Neither mowing nor paying a contractor automatically releases a government lien.

Request mowing, brush clearing or debris removal and include the deadline. We also offer recurring maintenance to help keep the same conditions from returning.
